01 · Practice

A gaze, a tree, a circle—three ways of looking.

Across portraiture, landscape and abstraction, recurring forms become emotional anchors: the directness of an eye, the solitude of a tree, the infinite pull of a circle. Together, the works invite a slower and more attentive kind of seeing.

Selected works

A journey through recurring subjects—identity, memory, resilience and the spaces we inhabit alone and together.

Landscape · Atmosphere

The Solitary Tree

A lone tree stands between earth and an expansive, changing sky. Birds scatter across the horizon while warm light gathers at the edge of day—a meditation on endurance, distance and quiet renewal.

Abstraction · Connection

Held in the Circle

Curved bodies meet hard-edged fields of colour. The circle acts as shelter, eye and orbit—holding two figures in a composition that balances tension with tenderness.

Texture · Perception

What the Eye Keeps

Thousands of small marks gather into a face. From nearby, the surface is rhythm and colour; from a distance, it becomes a presence looking back. Perception completes the portrait.

Survivors

An elder and child emerge from the dark in a spare monochrome vocabulary. The work turns closeness into resistance: care becomes the gesture through which hardship is endured.

02 · The artist

Bibhuti Bhusan
Mohapatra

Bibhuti Bhusan Mohapatra’s practice moves freely between figurative painting, atmospheric landscapes, bold geometric compositions and intimate works from the archive. Across these modes, the work returns to a shared concern: how a single image can hold memory, vulnerability and hope.
